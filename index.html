Qi Huang

M.Sc. student in Computational and Applied Mathematics, The University of Chicago

I am a master’s student in Computational and Applied Mathematics at the University of Chicago. Before Chicago, I received my B.Sc. in Mathematics from the University of Liverpool with First Class Honours, ranked in the top 1% of my major, after beginning my undergraduate studies in Applied Mathematics at Xi’an Jiaotong-Liverpool University.

My research spans statistics and machine learning. At UChicago, I work with Prof. Xinran Li on causal inference for matched observational studies — randomization inference under covariate-adaptive designs and sensitivity analysis under hidden bias. At Princeton’s Zhuang Lab, I build scientific world models that predict the outcomes of candidate research actions to guide autonomous experiment planning with agents and vision-language models.

At UChicago’s Knowledge Lab, working with Prof. James A. Evans, I led the mathematical modeling for a large-scale study of how AI breakthroughs concentrate scientific attention and erode intellectual diversity — drawing on 7.18 million papers, 73,000 peer reviews, and 3.7 million scientists across 36 years. The study has been submitted to Science. I also work with Prof. Xiao Luo at the University of Wisconsin–Madison on audio-visual test-time adaptation under severe distribution shift.
