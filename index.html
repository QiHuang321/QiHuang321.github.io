Sensitivity analysis for matched observational studies

Nov 2025 – present

Department of Statistics, University of Chicago

Mentor: Prof. Xinran Li

A studentized sensitivity analysis for Neyman’s weak null in matched observational studies.

Details
  • Anchors the within-set assignment law at fitted propensity odds rather than the uniform law.
  • The resulting Horvitz–Thompson lower envelope is mean-controlled and pointwise sharp, with asymptotically valid studentized tests; it extends in closed form to full matching and is applied to a right heart catheterization study.

Machine learning for science

Scientific world models & LLM agents

Mar 2026 – present

Zhuang Lab, Department of Computer Science, Princeton University

Mentor: Prof. Zhuang Liu

A scientific world model that predicts the outcomes of candidate research actions (research state + action → predicted outcome) to guide autonomous experiment planning with agents and vision-language models.

Details
  • Mechanism-aware predictors and a test-time selective-adaptation router for out-of-distribution generalization across unseen action families.
  • Reduced planner regret by ~80% in held-out replay evaluations.

Computational social science

Sep 2025 – Jul 2026

Knowledge Lab, University of Chicago

Mentor: Prof. James A. Evans

Led the mathematical modeling for a large-scale study of how AI breakthroughs concentrate scientific attention and erode intellectual diversity.

Details

Audio-visual test-time adaptation

Sep 2025 – Jun 2026

Department of Statistics, University of Wisconsin–Madison

Mentor: Prof. Xiao Luo

An audio-visual test-time adaptation framework for severe distribution shift that gates each modality with a binary accept/reject decision rather than softmax-style weighting.

Details
  • Learned attention maps with entropy-adaptive token dropout, curriculum annealing and class-specific thresholds reduce overfitting to noisy pseudo-labels.
