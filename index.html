Causal inference

Nov 2025 – present

Sensitivity analysis for matched observational studies

Department of Statistics, University of Chicago

Mentor: Prof. Xinran Li

Working with Prof. Xinran Li on how residual propensity-score differences and hidden bias affect causal inference in matched observational studies.

Details
  • Develops a covariate-adaptive, studentized sensitivity analysis for Neyman’s weak null, allowing heterogeneous treatment effects in matched pairs and full matching.
  • Uses bounded perturbations of baseline propensity odds to construct a Horvitz–Thompson lower envelope. Validity is conditional on the within-set assignment law, with additional endpoint-coverage or rate conditions for estimated propensities.
  • Studies balanced comparison weights and their design sensitivity under a specified favorable model. Working paper.

Sep 2025 – Jul 2026

Computational social science

Knowledge Lab, University of Chicago

Mentor: Prof. James A. Evans

Co-designed and implemented agent-based simulations for a study of AI breakthroughs, scientific attention, and intellectual diversity.

Details
  • The study analyzes 7,180,635 computer-science papers, 73,089 peer reviews, and topic shifts among 3,707,765 researchers across 36 years.
  • Stylized simulations examine how reviewer assignments tied to submission load can concentrate accepted topics and reduce diversity among accepted authors.
  • Co-authored the resulting manuscript, submitted to Nature Machine Intelligence; preprint available on arXiv.

Sep 2025 – Jun 2026

Audio-visual test-time adaptation

Department of Statistics, University of Wisconsin–Madison

Mentor: Prof. Xiao Luo

HEART checks pseudo-label reliability through complementary audio–visual routes before adapting to an unlabeled target stream.

Details
  • Two half-routes combine source audio with adapted video, and adapted audio with source video; agreement with the anchor pseudo-label supplies a detached reliability signal.
  • A batch gate based on support coverage and contrast downweights unsupported samples. When batch evidence is uninformative, the update recovers the Self-Anchor objective.
  • On AVRobustBench at severity 5, mean accuracy improves from 46.98% to 48.90% over Self-Anchor on VGGSound-2C, while remaining similar on KS32-2C (65.73% to 65.75%).
